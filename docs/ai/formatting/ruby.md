# Ruby (`lang-ruby`)

## Parser
Prism (the parser of CRuby 3.4+; Ruby 3.3 to 4.0 syntax; `ruby-prism-sys` pinned `=2.1.0`, the library's C sources built by `cc`, bumped by hand) behind `crates/lang-ruby/src/adapter.rs`, for parsing only: layout and rules are ours. The Rust crate `ruby-prism` is not a dependency: it wraps neither the lexer's token callback nor a way to build its result from our own parser call. `adapter/ffi.rs` (the crate's only `unsafe`, allowed by a `deny` in its manifest) makes one parse with the C library's lex callback set and then visits the tree with the library's visitor: tokens and node spans of the same parse, no second pass. The adapter drops newlines, comments and the separators of word lists, which `provider_api::adapter::replay` rebuilds from the text between tokens (`#` comments, `=begin` blocks, a `\` that continues a line); `__END__` and the text after it are one `Data` token that the formatter keeps byte for byte (`exact_end: [Data]`).

Why Prism, measured against tree-sitter-ruby 0.23.1 and lib-ruby-parser 4.0.6 on the same 18,958 files (86.5 MB; aarch64 Graviton, release, parse only): parse errors 0, 5 and 15; speed 124-178, 7-11 and 19-28 MB/s; Prism is the reference for what CRuby accepts (it rejects `a[&]`, which 3.4 forbids; tree-sitter accepts it) and follows each Ruby release (ruby/prism, MIT, CRuby core team); lib-ruby-parser stops at Ruby 3.1.

`grammars/ruby.grammar` has a row per Prism node type (`CallNode` is `Call`, `SelfNode` is `This`; the implicit block parameters `it` and `_1`, which Prism spans over the whole block, have none) and opts into `roles`: 67 kinds have a role, 83 are `#opaque` (table in [../architecture/syntax-tree.md](../architecture/syntax-tree.md)). Prism makes operators and index reads method calls, so `a + b` and `a[1]` are `Call`s (`#call` and `#member-access`) and only `and`, `or` and ranges are `#binary`; `if`, `unless` and the ternary are `If`/`Unless` and `#opaque`. Two places where Prism's spans do not follow the text, which the adapter corrects:
- A method or block body with `rescue` is a `Begin` that Prism spans over the whole method or block; the adapter makes it span its clauses.
- A heredoc's body comes after the rest of its opening line, and Prism ends the nodes of that line before it. The body (text, interpolations, closing label, without the line break after it) is one `HeredocBody` token with no nodes of its own, and the nodes that hold the whole line are extended over it, so it is a child of the innermost of them.

Files: `*.rb`, `*.rake`, `*.gemspec`, `*.ru`, `Gemfile`, `Rakefile`, `Guardfile`, `Capfile`.

## Formatting
The reference is RuboCop's Layout department (`rubocop --only Layout -a`, default configuration, 1.92.3, Ruby 3.4; `scripts/parity.sh format rubocop`: RuboCop runs once over a copy of the corpus, its changed files are cached in `.cargo/tmp/rb`, keyed by version, command and file contents). RuboCop keeps every line break of the source and corrects blanks, blank lines and indentation, so the formatter is line-preserving: `core_format::stream` (the token-line stream PowerShell shares) with `crates/lang-ruby/format.rules` as its only code (the fact `source_indent`: each line keeps the indentation it has; `continuation = \`). What it sets:
- Blank lines: at most `max_blank_lines` (1, `Layout/EmptyLines`); the blank lines at the start of the file stay, and a file of only blank lines keeps one line break (the source-indent stream). `core_format::stream_blank` (the `blanks` facts of `format-rows` in `lang.yaml`: kind sets and words) decides the lines of statements: a blank line after a guard clause (a modifier `if` or `unless`, or a block form without `else`, whose one statement is a jump on one line: `return`, `break`, `next`, `raise`; none after a guard that is followed by another guard or jump), around a bare `private`, `protected`, `public` or `module_function`, after the last of the magic comments at the top of the file and between definitions (not between two definitions that each fit one line); none at the start of a `class`, `module`, `def` or block body and none before its closing `end` or `}` (comments in the body keep their blank lines). A `# rubocop:` directive that follows an `end` directly stays with it.
- Indentation: `core_format::stream_indent` (the `indents` facts). The first line of each statement of a body stands one `indent_size` in from the line the body opens on (a block: the line its call starts on, or the line of its dot when the call continues on a line of its own); `end`, `}` and the branch keywords (`else`, `elsif`, `rescue`, `ensure`, `when`) stand where that line stands; a body that opens in the middle of a line (`x = if c`) stays as it is. A dot that starts a line stands under the first dot of the chain when that is on the line the chain starts on, under the start of a chain that starts in the middle of a line, else one step in from the statement or hash pair the chain belongs to; chains inside arguments, arrays and parentheses stay. A list whose bracket ends its line (`(`, `[`, `{`; arguments, array and hash elements) has its first element one step in from the line of the bracket, later elements under it and the closer at the line of the bracket; not when the bracket sits in a call whose first argument is on the same line (the list stands where the call's arguments stand), and the lines of a `%w[` list stay. The second operand of an `&&` or `||` in a condition stands under the first. Every other line moves by the amount of the statement it belongs to; a line inside a multi-line literal never moves.
- Blanks between tokens of a line (`[space]`): none before `,` and `;`, none inside `( )`, inside `[ ]` of an array and in `#{ }`, one inside the braces of a hash and a block (none in an empty pair); `[at_least_one]` adds a blank after `,` and `;` and around `=`, so blanks that line up with a neighbouring line (`Layout/ExtraSpacing`) stay.
- A heredoc body, a string, a regex and `__END__` data are tokens that span lines: they are written as they are. A `\` that continues a line stays at the end of its line.

The leg has 18,958 files and no input is left out: RuboCop leaves 13,321 of 18,958 files as they are, and 16,302 are equal now (the verbatim files, the blank lines, the indentation of bodies, chains, conditions, heredocs and hanging lists). 32 files that RuboCop leaves as they are change (blocks and chains that RuboCop leaves where the source has them, aligned blanks inside a hash). A second format changes 0 files. 5,637 files change under RuboCop; 7 of them change in meaning (the cops that shift lines move the lines inside a multi-line string, regex or heredoc, and `TrailingWhitespace` empties the blanks in a `<<-` heredoc) and one ends in an infinite loop: these 8 are `tool-wrong` with their proof in `tests/parity/rubocop/differences.yaml`. The causes of the other changed files, in the order that completes most files (upper bounds of files equal): alignment to a column (argument, pair and parameter alignment), token spacing (17,153), heredoc indentation (17,770), comment text and trailing whitespace (18,001), line length (18,651), brace layout (18,958). Blank-line rows over kinds alone (`Def`/`Class` pairs) cost more files than they win (one-line definitions next to each other stay compact): the decision needs the node's shape, not only its kind. Plan: [../design/ruby.md](../design/ruby.md).

## Lint
The rules are RuboCop's Lint department (catalog `docs/ai/parity/rubocop.yaml`, every Lint cop of RuboCop 1.92.3 with its status). Three kinds, all on the tree of the grammar:
- Scope rules (`scope.rows`, a table for `core_rules::scope_rules`, and the parameters left alone in `src/rules/scope.rs`; [../rules/semantics.md](../rules/semantics.md#scope-rules-core_rulesscope_rules)): `unused-local-variable` (Lint/UselessAssignment), `unused-method-argument`, `unused-block-argument`, `underscore-prefixed-variable-used`. Ruby has no declaration syntax: a write declares, a block parameter shadows, a `#{x}` in a heredoc body reads `x` (the body is one token), and the shorthand `f(x:)` reads `x`.
- Shared bug rules on the roles (`no-assignment-in-condition`: also `a && b = c`).
- The `check` family (`rules/checks.yaml` names a check, `src/rules/*.rs` holds the checks, one Rust function on the node kinds it lists): rules that read the shape of a call, a class or a clause. Operators are calls here (`x == x` is a `Call`), a `do` block belongs to the outer call, and the root is the only `Program` node (the adapter drops Prism's).

Coverage of RuboCop's default Lint findings on the corpus below, by rule and line (`scripts/parity.sh coverage rubocop`, installs a portable Ruby and the gem into `<target>/parity/`): the share is in the Language support table with `preset:ruby/rubocop` (Sudsy's own defaults keep only the bug cops on). What is missing is named in the catalog: cops with no rule (`planned`; the ones RuboCop disables or marks pending are `deferred`), and partial coverage of Lint/UselessAssignment (382 of 573: an assignment overwritten before any read, which the binder does not model) and Lint/SelfAssignment (`a, b = a, b`). Findings only Sudsy reports on the corpus (47 in all) are `# rubocop:disable` comments that Sudsy does not read (all 12 `boolean-symbol`, `unknown-rule-in-directive`), a multi-line condition reported at its first line while RuboCop reports the assignment line (`no-assignment-in-condition` 9), and a few unexplained (`unused-local-variable` 8 of ~390, `ambiguous-block-association` 8, `useless-method-definition` 4, `empty-when` 3, `no-broad-catch` 3). Parameters are left alone when the method yields (`&block`), calls `binding`, or is the receiver of `def p.x` / `class << p`; locals and parameters starting with `_` are not unused.

## Conformance
`tests/conformance/fetch-ruby-corpus.sh` fetches Rails, Discourse, RuboCop and Jekyll at pinned commits (`.rb` and `.rake` files, 18,958 files): every file parses without error, prints back byte for byte, its tree fits the grammar (`ShapeChecker`) and it formats with the same tokens and idempotently (`cargo test --profile corpus -p lang-ruby --test integration -- --ignored --nocapture conformance`, 34 s). Parse speed per repository: `cargo test --release -p lang-ruby --test integration -- --ignored --nocapture speed` ([../performance/numbers.md](../performance/numbers.md)).
