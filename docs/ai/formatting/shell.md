# Shell (`lang-shell`)

## Parser
tree-sitter-bash 0.25.1 (MIT, tree-sitter organization, Bash grammar), forked in `grammars/tree-sitter-bash` (`FORK.md` lists the changes: `for x do`, a word ending in `$`, `a=1 b=2` + line break + a command, a quoted string in a case pattern, a here-string after another redirect; [../development/grammar-forks.md](../development/grammar-forks.md)), linked in natively and converted by `core-treesitter` ([../architecture/adding-a-language.md](../architecture/adding-a-language.md)); the node mapping is the `mapping` of `crates/lang-shell/lang.yaml`, the tree shape `grammars/shell.grammar`. One grammar serves POSIX sh and Bash: a POSIX script is parsed as Bash. The adapter runs in composite text mode (`Composite { text: "Word", continuation: true, tolerant: [...] }`): a leaf the mapping does not name (`declare`, the text of a string, the inside of `(( ))`) is a `Word`, a `\` that ends a line is whitespace trivia, and a syntax error tree-sitter reports below an assignment, test, string, concatenation or substitution is not reported (tree-sitter-bash mis-parses a quoted string in a `=~` pattern; the text is valid and kept as written).

The tree is statements as nodes (`Command`, `Pipeline`, `List` (`&&`, `||`), `Redirected`, `Negated`, `Subshell`, `Group` (`{ }`), `If`/`Elif`/`Else`, `While`, `For`, `CFor`, `DoGroup`, `Case`/`CaseItem`, `Function`, `HeredocRedirect`) and the nodes of a word: `Assign` (`name=value`, also in a declaration), `Test` (`[ ... ]`, `[[ ... ]]`), `String` (`"..."`: text pieces, expansions and substitutions), `Concat` (adjacent parts of one word, `a$(b)"c"`), `Subst` (`$(...)` or backticks, holding the `Command` nodes inside; `<(...)` too), `Array` (`( b c )`, holding the element words) and `Redirect` (`> file`, `2>&1`, `<<< word`: operator and target), over flat tokens: `Keyword`, `Op` (`&`, `&&`, `||`, `|`, `|&`), `CaseEnd` (`;;`, `;&`, `;;&`; `#optional`, the last arm may gain one), `Semi` (`;`, ignored by the safety check), `Word` (a word without quotes or substitutions, the text pieces of a string, `${...}` and `$var`), `Arith` (`$(( a + 1 ))`), `HeredocOp`/`HeredocBody`/`HeredocEnd`. Single-quoted strings and `$'...'` are one `Word`.

Files: `*.sh`, `*.bash`, `.bashrc`, `.bash_profile`, `.profile`. A file without an extension is not recognized by its `#!` line: file selection matches names and extensions only, and sniffing content for this one language is not worth a core feature.

## Formatting
Layout follows shfmt's defaults (tabs, no flags) except the three options the `shfmt` preset sets (`comment_column`, `heredoc_indent`, `case_last_terminator`).
- One statement per line; `a; b` on one line becomes two lines; a `;` before a line break goes; `a &` keeps `&` after the statement. Blank lines between statements, after an opener and before a closer are kept up to `max_blank_lines`.
- `if cond; then`, `elif cond; then`, `while cond; do`, `for x in words; do`: `then` and `do` move to the head line (`; then`); bodies are indented one level; `fi`, `done`, `}`, `)` close at the head's column. A compound command written on one line with at most one statement per body (`if a; then b; fi`, `{ a; }`, `foo() { :; }`) stays on one line; any body with several statements, or a source with line breaks, is laid out on lines.
- `function`: `name() {` (the `{` joins the line of the name), `function name {` keeps the keyword.
- `case`: patterns at the column of `case`, alternatives as `a | b)`, the body indented, `;;` on its own line at the body's indentation. An arm written on one line (`a) b ;;`) stays on one line when it has at most one statement. With `case_last_terminator: add` a last arm without `;;` gets one.
- Pipelines and lists: `a && b`; a line break after `&&`, `||` or `|` is kept and the rest indented one level; an operator behind a line continuation (`a \` + line break + `&& b`) moves to the end of the previous line, as shfmt does. A continuation between words is kept (`\` at the end of the line, the next line indented one level).
- Redirects: no blank after the operator (`2> f` is `2>f`) unless the word would run into it (`< <(cmd)`); a redirect keeps its place among the words.
- Heredocs: `<<EOF` / `<<-EOF` and the rest of the line are laid out; the body and the delimiter line are printed as written, at column 0, after the line. With `heredoc_indent: reindent` (tab indentation) a `<<-` body loses its leading tabs and is indented one level below the command's line, the delimiter as deep as the command (`HeredocBody` and `HeredocEnd` are `#restyle`: the safety check ignores leading tabs there).
- Comments: own-line comments stay on their lines at the indentation of what follows; a comment before `else`, `elif`, `fi`, `done`, `}` or `;;` goes into the body above it when it stands deeper than the keyword, else it stands at the keyword's column (shfmt's rule). A trailing comment keeps the blanks the source has before it (`comment_column: keep`); with `align` it gets one blank, or the blanks that put it in one column with the trailing comments of the lines right above and below (one past the widest text before a comment, tabs counted as one; a line without a trailing comment ends the run), the way shfmt aligns.
- Substitutions and arrays: `$(...)` holds its commands on one line (`$(a | b)`, no blanks inside) unless it has several commands or the source breaks the line after `$(` or before `)`; then one command per line, indented one level from the line of the `$(`, `)` on its own line. The same inside strings. A `$(...)` with a heredoc or a continuation, and backticks, are kept as written. Array elements stay on the lines of the source; the lines after the first break are indented one level, a break after `(` or before `)` stays, no blanks inside `( )`.
- Not changed: words, `$(( ))`, `(( ))`, `[[ ]]`, `[ ]` and `<(...)` (but the blanks inside `(( ))`, `for (( ))` and `$(( ))` go: `((a))`; the operators inside are spaced like shfmt does: `((i = 0))`, `$((a + 1))`, `for ((i = 0; i < n; i++))`; a subscript and `i++ ))` are kept as written). The safety check allows only that and the blanks of redirects to differ from the input: every other token keeps its kind and text.

shfmt is the comparable tool; `scripts/parity.sh format shfmt` compares the output ([../development/parity.md](../development/parity.md)). Its flags map to nothing yet: `-i N` is `indent_style: space` + `indent_size: N`; `-bn`, `-ci`, `-sr`, `-fn` are deferred (catalog `docs/ai/parity/shfmt.yaml`).

## Presets
`shell/shellcheck`: turns on the rules that have a shellcheck equivalent ([../parity/shellcheck.yaml](../parity/shellcheck.yaml)) at shellcheck's severities (error stays error; warning, info and style become `warn`).

`shell/shfmt`: tabs, `comment_column: align`, `heredoc_indent: reindent`, `case_last_terminator: add`; the format parity run uses it.

## Rules
Shared: suppression comments (`# sudsy-disable-next-line <rule>`), `no-empty-comment`, `comment-space`, `max-line-length`. The grammar has no `#mapping` or `#sequence` nodes, so the key rules do not apply. The language rules are a shellcheck subset, listed in [../rules/builtin.md](../rules/builtin.md); the catalog maps each SC code to its rule or says why it is deferred.
- Defaults: the quoting and command-misuse rules are on (not the word-shape rules of `words.yaml`, which the preset turns on) (`warn`, `quote-at-expansion` `error`); `declare-and-assign-separately` and the info and style rules are `off`. The preset turns on every rule at shellcheck's severities.
- Forms: pattern files in `crates/lang-shell/rules/` (`commands.yaml`, `quoting.yaml`); `quote-command-substitution`, `no-backticks` and `declare-and-assign-separately` match `Subst` and `Assign` nodes; `quote-variable`, `quote-at-expansion` and `quote-star-expansion` use `token:` (a regex on the word tokens and `Concat` nodes of a command or `[ ]`, [../rules/patterns.md](../rules/patterns.md#tokens)); `cd-without-exit` and `useless-echo` in Rust (`src/rules.rs`); `unused-variable` (`SC2034`) and `unassigned-variable` (`SC2154`, both `off` by default, on in the preset) are `family: scope` rows over the shared scope binder (`scope.rows`, `src/scope.rs`: the file is one scope, `Program` has the `#scope` tag; `Assign`, `for` and the operands of `read`, `local`, `declare`, `export`, `getopts`, `printf -v` write; `$x`, `${x...}`, heredoc bodies and `$(( ))` are reads from the token text; an assignment in the environment of a command or after `export` counts as used; `IFS`, `PATH`, `PS1` and the like are never unused; only lower case names are reported unassigned, once). The commands inside a `$(...)` are `Command` nodes, so every rule runs on them, and so do the embedded shell rules of a Dockerfile `RUN`.
- Syntax only: quoting is judged from the word's text, so `quote-variable` reads the arguments of a command and the words of `[ ]`, not the command's name, assignments, loop lists or `[[ ]]`, and cannot know a variable holds a number or one word (shellcheck follows literal assignments, so it stays silent on `x=3; [ $x -lt 4 ]`). A word with a quoted string that holds a nested `$(`/`${` ends the scan of that word, so such a word is not judged. Data flow across functions and subshells, the target dialect (`SC3xxx`) and knowledge of other commands' options are deferred.

## Parity
`scripts/parity.sh shellcheck` compares the rules with shellcheck 0.11.0 (`-o all`, so the optional checks count) on the rule fixtures; see [../development/parity.md](../development/parity.md). Over the corpus below the rules agree with shellcheck on all 116 findings of covered codes; of the 125 Sudsy-only ones, 116 are `quote-variable` (95 in nvm.sh) on variables shellcheck knows hold a literal or a number and 9 are in places shellcheck does not report (`useless-echo` of a multi-line substitution, `no-which` and `quote-command-substitution` inside `[ ]`, `echo` and `$(...)` in a string, `cd-without-exit` in `$(cd d; pwd)`, `and-or-is-not-if-else` on `a && (b || c)`).

## Conformance
`tests/conformance/fetch-shell-corpus.sh` fetches shell scripts at pinned commits: the install scripts of dotnet, nvm, rustup, docker and Homebrew, the docker-library entrypoints of postgres and mysql, and the Bash sources of pyenv, bats-core and asdf. `cargo test --profile corpus -p lang-shell --test integration -- --ignored --nocapture conformance` checks that each file that tree-sitter parses (all 81) round-trips, formats with the same tokens (the safety check, comments compared as well) and formats idempotently. `tests/parity/shfmt/` compares with shfmt on the same files: 71 of 82 (the 81 corpus files and the `layout.sh` fixture) are identical, the 11 others are named in `tests/parity/shfmt/differences.yaml`. A file with a syntax error runs no rule.

The run compares `sudsy fix` with the rule `no-backticks` on (`tests/parity/shfmt/sudsy.yaml`, with the `shell/shfmt` preset): shfmt rewrites backticks to `$(...)`, which the formatter never does (it never changes a token) and the rule's safe fix does for a body without a backslash.

## Continued mode
A host that writes every line break of the code as a `\` continuation (a Dockerfile `RUN`) embeds Shell with `EmbedMode::Continued` and the host's `indent_style`, `indent_size`, `binary_operator_line`, `space_redirects` and `indent_case_arms` (`core_format::Forward`). Shell writes the continuation itself: the printer (`PrintOptions::continuation`) ends every line of code with ` \`, a blank line with `\` alone and a whole-comment line with nothing, and never touches a line inside a multi-line quote (`Doc::Verbatim`). Every line after the first is one level in; the first statement, when it is a chain of commands, has the lines from its first operator break on at the level the chain gives them (`is_first_break` in `flow`). Blank lines are all kept (each is a line of the text). The layout drops the `;` before `;;` and before the `)` of a subshell and the last `;`, keeps a `;` that ends a line of a compound command, and writes a pipeline after `&&` or `||` with the first broken `|` level with the operator and later ones one level deeper.

Comment lines: Docker drops every `#` line after the first of a continued instruction, quotes or not. `Engine::parse_continued` (core-treesitter) blanks them for the parser (spaces and a `\`, so the line joins its neighbours) and seeds them as comment tokens at their offsets, so the tree has them as comments and Shell prints them through its normal comment path. The host takes the text laid out only when its comment lines equal the source's; else the text stays as written.
