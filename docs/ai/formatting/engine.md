# Formatting engine (`core-format`)

Provider formatting code turns the tree into a layout document with shared shapes; a shared Prettier-style printer fits it to the width. Formatting code reads the resolved options directly (no spec layer). A node with no code of its own uses its grammar tag's shape (`#block`, `#delimited-list`, ...).

## Member chains (`shapes::call_chain`)
One layout for `a.b().c().d()` in every language with roles. It walks the node by roles and `Grammar::slot` (`#call`: `callee`, `arguments`; `#member-access`: `object`, `member`; `#index`; `#unary`: `operand`; the receiver is `object`, else `callee`, else `operand`; `#transparent` parentheses on the way are looked through when the language says so) and cuts the links into groups. `ChainPolicy::of(options)` reads the policy from the language's options (all declared in its `lang.yaml`, so a preset can set them; undeclared ones take neutral values): `member_chain_style` (`fluent`: one group per call, the first call stays on the head's line for a short or type-like head, `member_chain_head_calls`, `member_chain_keep_accesses`; `grouped`: first group then call groups, labelled `member-chain`; `soft` (Python): the head keeps the accesses before its first call, every access after a call or index starts a group behind the hook's `separator` in one group of the chain's own, `member_chain_min_groups` groups after the first needed), `member_chain_min_calls`, `member_chain_short_head`, `member_chain_break_on_block`, `member_chain_indent`. What a language prints itself goes through `ChainHooks` (`element`, `link`, `through`, `simple_argument`, `statement`): JS prints parentheses, comments and `[expr]` links, Java the arguments of the head call.

## Operator chains (`shapes::binary_chain`)
One layout for `a + b + c` and `a && b || c` in every language with `#binary-chain` kinds (fields `left`, `operator`, `right`). Operands of one precedence level (the grammar's `precedence` lines) flatten into one chain, down the left side or, for a `right` operator (C# `??`), the right side. `BinaryPolicy::of(options)`: `binary_operator_line` (`start`: operator first, one operand per line; `end`: operator ends the line, each step its own group unless the chain mixes node kinds), `binary_indent` (levels of the continuation lines), `indent_nested_binary` (a chain inside a chain of another precedence indents), `binary_hug` (`assignment`, `statement`: the chain leaves its indentation to an assignment, declaration, entry or `return`/`throw` around it, found through roles). What a language decides itself goes through `BinaryHooks` (`place`: bare inside parentheses it prints, flush, wrapped below an opening line, or indented; `inline_right`, `tail_outside`, `outer`/`inner` for transparent parentheses, `flattens`, `token`, `comment_ends_line`, `reattach_comments`): JS places a chain by its parent kind, C# lists the layouts that indent their own value.

## Assignments (`shapes::assignment`)
One layout for `left = right`, declarators and entries: Prettier's set (break-after-operator, never-break, fluid, break-lhs, chain, chain-tail, plus break-always after a line comment on the operator). `AssignPolicy::of(options)`: `assignment_layout` (`compact`: the right side stays on the operator's line; `fluid`: it moves when its first line does not fit, and the chain layouts of `a = b = c` apply; `break`: it moves when the whole does not fit), `assignment_conditional` (a conditional on the right always moves, or only one whose test is an operator chain), `assignment_indent`. Roles choose first: a line comment on the operator, a chain, a comment on a line of its own in front of the right side, a `#binary-chain` right side, a `#conditional`. What the language judges comes through `AssignHooks` (`layout`: its own rules before the style's default, `right_doc`, `chain_parent`, `chain_tail`, `inline_binary`, `outer`/`inner`): JS keeps Prettier's predicates (strings, short member chains, templates, `require`, arrow chains, destructuring), C# its strings, collection expressions and property initializers, Java an array initializer or lambda that stays on the line.

## Conditionals (`shapes::conditional`)
One layout for `test ? a : b` (`#conditional` with `condition`, `then`, `else`; the `?` and `:` are the tokens in front of the last two). `conditional_layout`: `indented` (the test, then both branches `conditional_indent` levels below, each conditional a group of its own) or `chained` (Prettier's: branches align after `? ` and `: `, a chain in the `else` branch breaks as one, a conditional in a `then` branch is parenthesized when broken). `ConditionalHooks`: `wrap_branches`/`bare_branch` (JSX branches in parentheses), `around` (the places that indent the whole), `token`, `comment_ends_line`, `outer`/`inner`.

## Compound statements (`shapes::compound_body`, `compound_continuation`)
The head of `if`, `while`, `for`, `do`, `with` is the language's; the body and the clauses after it are shared. A body of a kind tagged `#empty-statement` follows the head as it is (`;`), one tagged `#block` follows after a space, any other statement goes indented on the next line when it does not fit (`inline`: stays on the line, for `else if`; `Join::Hang`: on the next line when the enclosing group breaks; `Join::Fits`: the move is a group of its own, so the body joins the head when the whole fits; `Join::Never`: always on the next line). A continuation keyword (`else`, the `while` of `do`) joins the `}` of a block body with a space and stands on a line of its own after any other body or after a line comment; a blank line the source has before a comment in front of the keyword stays. JS and Java use them; C# and Kotlin use the generic sequence and brace options, PHP `[layout]` rows. Offside bodies (Python, YAML) are not on it yet.

## Content between delimiters (`shapes::between`, `enclosed`, `maybe_parenthesize`)
`between(open, inner, close)` is the content indented on lines of its own; `enclosed` puts it in a group; `maybe_parenthesize(inner)` is `enclosed` with parentheses that show only when the group breaks (ruff's `maybe_parenthesize`, Prettier's `return (...)`). Python, JS, Java, CSS and the shared conditional and binary-chain shapes build such layouts from them.

## Layout document and printer
- Primitives: `text`, `line`, `softline`, `hardline`, `group`, `indent`, `align` (`ALIGN_TO_COLUMN`: the lines start at the column the contents start at; `ALIGN_FROM_LINE` as a flag on the count: the count is from the indentation of the line the contents start on; `PrintOptions::align_with_tabs`: with tabs, an alignment is tabs for whole indent widths and spaces for the rest), `outdent` / `outdent_columns` (`Doc::Outdent`: the line starts that many levels or columns left of the indentation, `usize::MAX` levels is column 0), `if_break`, `fill`, `conditional_group`, `line_suffix`, `break_parent`, `Verbatim`, `Shared(Arc)` (O(1) clone, so conditional candidates do not copy), `LineStart`, `NewlineIfNeeded`, group ids with `indent_if_break` and `if_break_in(id, ..)` (tests another group; a group not yet printed counts as flat), `label(tag, doc)` (prints as its contents; a parent reads it with `Doc::label_of`, through `shared`), `line_suffix_boundary` (a hard line only when a line suffix is pending; does not break groups). `reflow(text, width)` (a `//` comment re-wrapped at the width with `//` continuation lines), `reserve(n)` (no output; counts `n` columns in the fit test, for a trailing comment), `fits_below(levels, width, fits, otherwise)` (prints `fits` when its probe fits on a line `levels` indents deeper, else `otherwise`), `shapes::tabular` (columns of a table the source lays out in rows, kinds parallel per column).
- Group fits flat if the rest of the line up to the next possible break fits. `conditional_group` takes the first candidate that fits (flat, forced breaks allowed); a candidate with a forced break is printed in break mode, so its groups are measured one by one; one whose every candidate has a forced break forces its parents. Line suffixes flush at the next newline; trailing whitespace is trimmed except protected verbatim output.
- Widths are Unicode display width. Text containing a newline breaks enclosing groups; only its first line counts when measuring.
- `format_tree` prints LF, then applies `final_newline` and the line ending: `end_of_line` `lf` or `crlf`, or (`auto`, also for a provider without the option) the file's first line break.
- Recursion depth equals source nesting: `FormatCtx::format_unguarded`, the C# member-chain walk, and `Doc::propagate_breaks` run through `core_format::deep` (`stacker`; it measures the stack every 32nd level), which continues on a fresh stack segment when the current one runs low, so layout depth is bounded by memory, not by a 2 MB debug thread stack. The printer and the safety comparison are iterative; `format_tree` frees the document with `Doc::dispose` (no recursive drop).

## Parentheses (`shapes::parens`)
A node tagged `#parenthesized` is `(`, one expression, `)`. `ParenPolicy { layout, unneeded }`: `Flat` prints `(expr)` and leaves breaking to the layout around it, `Group` lets the expression move to its own indented line (`paren_group`); `Keep` leaves every pair of the source, `Drop` leaves out a pair the hooks do not need (unless a comment inside ties it to its expression) and adds one to a node that needs it and the source lacks (`wrap`). `ParenHooks`: `needs` (the language's judgement, `Need::Yes/No/Keep`), `addable` (kinds that may get a pair), `tied` (a type cast comment). `binary_needs` decides an operator inside an operator from the grammar's `precedence` lines (lower binding level, same level on the right or not joining, and with `clarify` also `%` inside `+`/`-` and operands of bitwise operators). JS is `Flat` + `Drop` with Prettier's `needsParens` as the hook (statement-start, arrow body, `new` callee, casts, and so on stay in `lang-js`), C# is `Group` + `Keep`, Java keeps its parenthesized expressions as tokens of its own layouts.

## Blocks (`shapes::block`, `shapes::braces`)
`block` lays out `open`, items one per line, `close` over the node's items; `braces` puts the delimiters around a body a language laid out itself (Java's enum body). `BlockStyle`: `max_blank_lines`, `force_blank` (a rule for blank lines the source lacks: Java members), `empty` / `expand_empty` (`{}` or two lines; the language decides per node, JS and Java by the statement the block belongs to), `keep_same_line`, `keep_leading_blank` (a blank line after `{` stays: Java; not JS or C#). Tokens tagged `#stray` between the items (Java's `;`) are dropped. `flat` (the items are not indented: a C++ namespace) and `fit_single` (a body of one item stays on the line of its head when it fits) are the `[layout]` keys `flat=option:NAME=V` and `fit=option:NAME=V|W`; `fit` applies only to a body written on one line in the source.

## Shapes (`core-format::shapes`)
Delimited list (`ListLayout`: `Fit`; `PreserveBreak`, `PreserveFirstBreak` (a break after the opening delimiter, Prettier's objects), `PreserveItemBreak`, `MagicTrailingComma` (a separator after the last item, Black and ruff); `Fill` (items packed per line, Prettier's arrays of numbers; the trailing comma asks the list's group); `Break` always; `Flat` never breaks, as TOML inline tables), block, key-value, statement sequence, verbatim, binary chain (operator first on continuation lines), member chain (`shapes::call_chain`, below), and `delimited_list_between` / `block_between` for delimiters that are not first/last tokens. A shape finds its structure from the grammar: first/last significant token are delimiters, child nodes are items, `#separator` tokens after an item are its separator. List separators are re-created from `ListStyle`; block separators are kept. A child tagged `#list-prefix` is printed first. Blank lines between items are kept up to `max_blank_lines`; they vanish in a list that fits one line, unless `blank_breaks` (Prettier's objects and parameters). `ListStyle` also has `force_break` (the caller found a reason in the tree), `TrailingComma::AsSource`, `hang` (the items in a group of their own: after the opening delimiter on one line, else one per line; `inner_id` names that group, `id` the whole list, `indent_levels` the indentation), `rows` (items the source lays out in a table keep their rows, none with comments, `shapes::tabular`), `fill_below` (items all shorter than that in the source are filled; after a line comment a fill breaks), and tags: a `#hole` node (JS `Elision`) or a `#hole` separator with no item before it is an empty item, an item tagged `#final` (a rest element) takes no trailing comma. Inline block comments in front of the closing delimiter stay against it, the others stand on lines of their own. `shapes::arrow_body` lays out `head => body` of a lambda or switch rule (the body on the next line, `levels` in, when it does not fit; `own_group`: the body decides alone; `hug_block`: a block body follows the head, else it is the caller's) for Java and C#. `shapes::signature` lays out `<T> Type name(params) throws A` (a head that does not fit moves below the type parameters, clauses after the parameters go to their own line when the parameters broke; Java) and `parameter_list` breaks a parameter list from a minimum count (Kotlin).

Call arguments (`shapes::call_arguments`, `calls.rs`): `shapes::plan` reads the arguments' kinds and decides the layout: a function composition (more than one function argument, or an argument that is a call with a function argument; `CallSets::composition`, not under the parents of `no_composition`) breaks every argument onto a line of its own, and otherwise the last argument (option `hug_last_argument`: `last`, `sole` only when it is alone, `never`) or the first of two (`hug_first_argument`; the other argument must be short, `CallSets`) is hugged: `Args::layout` builds a conditional group of the flat candidate, the candidate whose hugged argument is printed broken, and every argument on its own line; blank lines between arguments, a comment before the `)` and a hugged argument next to one that breaks force the last. What can hug comes from tags: `#huggable` (an object or array with items), `#huggable-always` (a function), `#huggable-function` (a function or lambda whose body is a `#block`, a `#huggable-body`, an arrow function that hugs, or a `#huggable-tail` unless it is part of an arrow chain; not when its return type is a `#named-type` and its body an empty block), `#tail-wrapper` (a postfix `!` over a `#huggable-tail`), `#hug-wrapper` (a cast: hugs when its `#expression` child does), `#argument` (a node that holds one argument expression). The language prints the hugged argument itself (JS: the arrow in its `expand` layout) and passes it as `Expanded::Doc`. `[call]` rows of the rules file name callee sets and argument shapes (`callee=@set / StringLiteral,#function&block&params<=1 : test`) with the effects `flat` (the arguments stay on the call's line: `require("x")`, a multi-line template, test calls `it("does x", () => {})`), `test` (also keeps the parameters of a function argument on the line) and `hook` (`useEffect(() => {...}, [a])`); the language's `CallSets` (a Rust table, `lang-js` `call.rs`) are Prettier's simple call argument and the rest (`short`, `not_last`, concise arrays). A node of its own for the list (`ArgumentList`) goes through `shapes::call_arguments(cx, node, rows, style, list)`: a hugging argument of `style` gets the candidates (C#: `lambda_argument_hug`; with `sole` a lambda with an expression body hangs below its head), every other list is `list`. `shapes::keyword_argument` (`return`, `throw`: a binary chain `#binary-chain` in parentheses when it does not fit, a `#sequence` always) and `shapes::head_test` (the test of `if (`, `while (`; a negated chain keeps the head's line) read tags too.

## Sections, continuations and aligned entries (`shapes::section`, `shapes::continuation`)
`[layout]` row `section entries=K`: a header (its tokens and nodes) and the entries of kinds K one per line, no indentation, blank lines kept up to `max_blank_lines` (TOML tables, INI and EditorConfig sections); `document max_blank=option` keeps at most `max_blank_lines` between statements. `flow` prints the children with the spacing rows; `items` prints the node's child nodes one per line (as written when a token stands between them); `line_start` prints the node's text at column 0 after its comments (a preprocessor directive); with a kind set `relay` of an option (`align_escaped_newlines`) the text is collapsed (blanks outside literals and comments become one) and its trailing `\\` stand in one column (`relay.rs`), and a trailing comment follows `spaces_before_trailing_comments`; a node of a kind in the `break_after` set of an option (`break_template_declarations`: `yes`, `no`, `multiline`) puts a hard line after its `head` kind (`header_break.rs`), `multiline` joining the header to the rest when the rest up to its first possible break fits; a node of a kind in an option's `outdent`, `outdent_unless` or `flush_unless` set moves left by the columns of the option, one indent or to column 0 (`flush_unless`: one indent left when the option is true, column 0 when false) (comments in front of it separated by a blank line stay); `case` puts a block that is the only body on the line of the label; `compound` (kind sets `join` and `head` of an option) prints a head (`seq` of the children before the body), the body on the line (`Join::Fits`) or below, and `else` through `compound_continuation`; the `attach` kind sets of a `words` option (`brace_wrapping`) decide per word whether `{` stays on the line (`Flow::attach`; of several words for one kind the construct's own keyword picks); text values of `[space]` write `&` as `%amp`; `Outdent::Suffix` puts a comment that continues a trailing comment under it; `document` re-emits the blank lines at the start of the file when the bool option `keep_leading_blank_lines` is on. A `lang.yaml` option may carry `kinds: {role: [Kind, ..]}` (generated as `OPTION_KINDS`, given to `LazyRules::option_kinds`): the constructs it governs, for the roles `fit`, `no_fit_under`, `no_fit_with`, `flat`, `join`, `head`, `outdent`, `outdent_unless`, `flush_unless`, `tight` and `attach` (a map word to kinds); the block, compound, outdent and brace shapes read them. `key_value` row key `space_before` (a missing value is allowed; with a boolean option `align_comments` the trailing comments of consecutive `#key-value` entries start in one column, measured by `core-format/src/align.rs`). `shapes::continuation::Continued` is one logical line the source splits with `\` or a backtick (Dockerfile instructions, Makefile statements): the first physical line, then the indented rest, comment lines between kept, quotes kept as written; `FormatCtx::align_trailing_comment` does the same for `#statement` nodes (the first kind asked decides the run tag). `shapes::clause_break(items, ClauseBreak)`: a `: a, b` clause after a head, a group that stays on the line when it fits, else the colon before or after the first item and the commas before or after the lines (option values `before_colon`, `before_comma`, `after_colon`). `heredoc` prints a here-document body and its delimiter line as written at column 0 after the line (shell, Dockerfile); `as_written` keeps multi-line text as written (Makefile values and recipes). `ListStyle::unwrap` (wrapper node kinds whose children are the list's items: Protobuf `FieldOptions`), `ListStyle::break_from` (fewer items stay on the line however long, this many or more one per line, any comment inside expands) and `open_comments_own_line` (Protobuf options and literal lists).

## Words and inline children (`shapes::fill`)
`fill_children(parts, marker, text)` takes a run of `Part`s (a word or printed node, or a gap: soft, breakable, hard, or a kept space) in alternation, merges the gaps next to each other (a hard line beats a soft one), trims the ends, and gives `flat()` (everything on one line) and `filled()` (the contents of a `fill`, and whether a hard line forces the parent to break). A kept space prints as a space, or as `marker` and a soft line where the line breaks (JSX `{" "}`). `words` is a text as a fill with a line between words (HTML text), `runs` splits text at blanks and keeps the runs, `collapse_blanks` makes runs of blanks one space (Markdown prose). JSX children and HTML text use it. Markdown, YAML scalars and XML mixed content keep their lines as written (no reflow: roadmap), so they have no fill.

## Declarations (`shapes::declaration`) and `[blank]` rows
Read from tags, printed by the language's `Printer` (its element printer and spacing). `modifiers`: a `#modifiers` node whose leading `#annotation` nodes stand each on a line (`Annotations::Lines`), hang on a line of their own when the declaration does not fit on one (`Hanging`) or stay with the other modifiers (`Inline`; `Annotations::of`: annotations with arguments (a `#annotation-arguments` node with items) mean lines); an annotation without arguments that the file imports under a name of the option `type_use_annotations` (a glob list, Java) belongs to the type after it and stays on the line. `lead_annotations`: the leading `#annotation` elements (Java annotations, JS and Python decorators) each followed by a line break. `type_declaration`: modifiers, the head, the `#heritage` clauses and the `#block` body; `header` lays out a head and its clauses (`Clause`: separator and text) as one group with `HeaderStyle`: `grouped` (each clause moves to the next line alone), `continuation` levels, the group `id` of the whole header, and `first_on_own_line` (the first clause on a line of its own when the type parameters broke). TypeScript class and interface headers are on `header` too. `members`: one member per line, the blank lines the `[blank]` rows ask for. `enum_body`: `#enum-constant` nodes one per line, the `#stray` `;`, the `#enum-tail` members after a blank line.

`[blank]` rows (`core_format::blank`) say between which neighbouring members or statements a blank line is required although the source has none: `PREV / NEXT [: key=value ...]`, a row holds when every key does, any row decides. Keys test the next node, or the previous one with `prev.`: `parent`, `same_kind`, `has`, `lacks` (a child of a kind), `commented`, `doc` (a `/**` comment), `comment_blank` (a blank line between the comments in front), `annotated` (`any` or `args`: an annotation, or one with arguments, among the modifiers), `group`. Java (members: a blank unless both are fields without doc comment or annotation arguments) and Kotlin (files, class bodies, blocks) are rows; PHP uses them for statements.

## Literals (`core_format::literals`)
Text in, text out, with the language's policy as a style value. `string` re-quotes `'..'`/`".."` by the fewest quotes (`StringStyle`: preferred quote, keep a string that has its quote; the row's `nest` parts are copied as written; JS, CSS); `prefixed` is the rule of Black and ruff for prefixed and triple-quoted strings (fewest escapes, prefix spelling, f-string fields; `Quote::Preserve` leaves quotes); `number` spells a decimal number (`NumberStyle`: trailing fraction zeros kept, one kept or trimmed, `5.` as `5.0` or `5`, exponent zeros); `unescape_quotes` gives the value to compare after a quote change; `requote(from, to, text)` writes a token as a string of another row when that needs no more escapes (YAML single and double quoted scalars). What a string token is (delimiters, prefixes, escapes, doubled quotes) is the grammar's `string` row ([syntax-tree.md](../architecture/syntax-tree.md)): `prefixed` takes the row of the token, and the YAML quote change is `requote` between its rows. Comments are `Comment::text`.

## Layout cache
`FormatCtx::layout(node, flags, make)` builds the document of a node once per `(node, flags)` and returns it `shared`. A layout that prints a child twice (last call argument hugged or not, `conditional_group` candidates) calls it for the child, so nesting costs a constant factor per level, not 2^depth. `flags` is the provider's own numbering of the layouts it tries; nothing is cached unless a provider asks.

## Comments
Never positioned by the formatter: a comment is owned by a token (trivia rule) and printed with it (`FormatCtx::token`). Own-line comments stay on their own lines (`NewlineIfNeeded`); a block comment on the same line stays inline; a line comment after a token is a line suffix and breaks the group. Comments before a closing delimiter are dangling and printed inside the container. A second comment on a line already ending in a line comment moves to its own line. A list separator (also the one a trailing comma adds) is printed right after the last item, before the item's trailing comments (`FormatCtx::detach_trailing`, for every item of a list, not only the last); `hold_trailing`/`held_trailing` do the same for the whole run (a printed document may be cached while the comments are held back). `shapes::block_open_inside` moves a comment after the opening delimiter to a line of its own inside the block; `ListStyle::broken_separator` is the separator of a list that breaks, when it differs from `separator`; an item kept by an ignore comment ends with its own separator, which the list does not repeat. Structured trivia are comments: `Lines` (doc comments, line by line), `Directive` (`#verbatim-trivia`, kept at column 0; `#region` follows indentation).

## Layout directives (`DIRECTIVES_VERSION = 1`)
`BreakAll`, `KeepTogether`, `BreakBefore`/`BreakAfter`, `BlankLineBefore(n)`, `Align` (on the parent, only when expanded), `Preserve`, `Style(name)` (read by provider code only; none reads one yet). Rules request layout, the core performs it. Plugins cannot return layout documents. Text form (pattern files, plugin WIT, errors): `break-all`, `keep-together`, `break-before`, `break-after`, `blank-line-before N`, `align`, `preserve`, `style NAME`.

- Where honored: `BreakAll`/`KeepTogether` by every delimited list and by the C# member chain (on its outermost node: every call on its own line / never broken at the dots); `BreakBefore`/`BreakAfter`/`BlankLineBefore` by the gap between items of lists, blocks and statement sequences; `Align` by the key-value shape (the `#key-value` tag, JSON members); `Preserve` by every node.
- Conflicts (`BreakAll`+`KeepTogether`, `Preserve`+anything, differing `BlankLineBefore`/`Style`) are errors detected when added, naming both rules; the file is not formatted.
- Producers: every rule source (built-in, pattern `layout:`, Wasm plugin) through one path, `RuleContext::attach` (see [../rules/engine.md](../rules/engine.md#layout)). The `format` query reads the `layout(file)` query: the layout rules run in a pass of their own (syntax only, no semantic layers), so formatting depends on those rules' directives and not on the rest of the lint results; a lint-only config edit leaves `format` valid. With no layout rule in the language nothing runs.
- Levels and suppressions: an `off` rule attaches nothing; `sudsy-disable-next-line <rule>` silences the directives of nodes starting on the next line. `format`, `check`, the editor and fix-then-format all read the same query; the safety check (debug builds) applies to the result.

## Ignore regions
`sudsy-ignore` before a node keeps it verbatim (outermost node starting at that token); `sudsy-ignore-start` .. `sudsy-ignore-end` among the items of a list/block keeps those items verbatim; an unterminated start runs to the container's end. A marker is the comment body or its start followed by a blank; a role may list several markers in the grammar (`suppress ignore "sudsy-ignore"` and `suppress ignore "prettier-ignore"`), any of which counts, and the roles `ignore-start` / `ignore-end` / `ignore-line` (a trailing comment keeps its statement) take the foreign forms too (Python: `fmt: off`, `fmt: on`, `yapf: disable`, `fmt: skip`). `core_format::ignore` reads them (`mark_in`, `line_ignored`, `is_marker` for hosts with their own comment text like HTML, `IgnoreRun` for statement sequences, `region_end` for item lists).

## Consumed comments
A block that ends right before a token prints the deeper leading comments of that token itself; `FormatCtx::consume`/`consumed`/`remaining_comments` track what was printed, `lead` prints the rest in front of a statement or clause, `end_comments(last, col)` takes the comments at column `col` or deeper after a block (Python), `comments::indented_run`/`column`/`next_significant` serve the same decision for YAML collections and shell bodies, and `comment_lines` prints own-line comments one per line. GraphQL reads `# prettier-ignore`. C# honors it on statements, members and list items, not mid-expression.

## Safety check (debug builds, tests and the `corpus` profile)
`provider_api::format_file`: parse, refuse syntax errors, format. Release builds stop there and trust the formatter; debug builds (every test, and the corpus runs through the `corpus` cargo profile, release with debug assertions) then have `format_parsed` re-parse the output and compare node starts/ends and non-trivia tokens. Ignored in the comparison: trivia (structured trivia whole), `#separator` tokens (trailing commas may change; a wrongly removed one still changes structure), `#optional` tokens (the formatter may add or remove them; no shape or rule reads the tag), nodes tagged `#transparent` and their own tokens (JavaScript's parenthesized expression). Comments (trivia tagged `#comment`) are compared as a multiset of their texts for every language (line breaks as LF, the lines of a multi-line comment trimmed, the end of a one-line comment trimmed): a comment may move, not be lost, duplicated or edited. Tokens and nodes tagged `#restyle` must keep their position (their kind may change: YAML quotes, CSS `[a=x]` to `[a="x"]`), and `NodeFormatter::value(item)` (default: the text) must give the same `Cow<str>` for the input's and the output's item; a language puts the normalization the formatter applies (case, quotes, number spelling, blanks) there. The subtree of a `#restyle` node is compared through its value only. `NodeFormatter::extra_check(before, after)` is for what is not per item: YAML's data model, Markdown's block structure, JSX text. Any difference aborts that file with an internal error. Idempotency is a test property, not a runtime check.
With C# `preprocessor_symbol_sets` / `derive_symbol_sets`, `NodeFormatter::merge_variants` returns the output with inactive `#if` branches formatted from other parses ([csharp.md](csharp.md#inactive-if-branches)); `format_parsed` then runs the check once per parse that supplied text (reparse under that parse's symbols), in every build because it decides whether the merge is used, and falls back to the unmerged output on any failure.
## Source rewrites
A shared rule marked `format: true` (`Rule::formats`) has its fixes applied to the source before layout: `provider_api::format_parsed_with` runs the rules that the language has a `[<rule-id>]` section for in its `format.rules` (`NodeFormatter::rows`, also `RuleHooks::rows` in lint), with the format options (`FileContext::format`), applies the fixes (`core_rules::fixes`), parses the result again and repeats (`rewrite_rounds` plus one round for these rules). `run_file_rules` runs only `check_file`; `RuleContext::nodes` shares one node walk among the rules and `FileContext::parsed` keeps a rule's parsed rows per language. `modifier-order` (`core_rules::modifiers`, Java and Kotlin) is the first rule that uses it. A section needs at least two languages (see [../design/unification.md](../design/unification.md) W1, H1). Token rewrites that need no rule are `[rewrite]` rows (`core_format::rewrite`: PHP, JS, Java), applied by the language's `rewrite_source` through `RuleFormatter::rewrite_source`.

An option that reorders code (C# `sort_usings`) does it through `NodeFormatter::rewrite_source`: `format_parsed` takes the rewritten text, parses it again (a rewrite that does not parse is an internal error) and formats and checks that tree; the input tree is not compared with the output.

## Testing helper
`core_format::testing::check` enforces round trip, unchanged meaning, idempotency and comments kept. Scaling guard: `core-format/tests/scaling.rs` (steps of `fits` grow at most 4x for 4x the document).
